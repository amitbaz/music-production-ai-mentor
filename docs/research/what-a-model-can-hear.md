# Research — what a model can hear, and what numbers can stand in for hearing

Date: 2026-10-15 · Status: research note, not a decision · Resolves [#53](https://github.com/amitbaz/music-production-ai-mentor/issues/53), part of the listening map [#50](https://github.com/amitbaz/music-production-ai-mentor/issues/50)

The question, in three parts: (1) for each provider and model that could be on the supported list (a context window of at least 1,000,000 tokens, [ADR 0019](../adr/0019-only-supported-models-with-a-1m-window.md)), what its own documentation says about audio input; (2) which numeric descriptions of audio are known to let a language model judge a mix; (3) what computes those numbers and what each option costs.

The earlier note [hearing-live-audio-and-where-the-agent-runs.md](hearing-live-audio-and-where-the-agent-runs.md) took Gemini's mono, 16 kbps downsampling as a given to design around. This note replaces that part of it. The model is interchangeable; the current provider is Gemini and may change to fit what the mentor needs to hear.

Docs were read on 2026-10-15. The supported list today holds one model, `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (`packages/harness/src/providers/gemini.ts`).

Markers: **[verified]** read in the linked primary source during this research · **[paper]** a published paper, reported as it states it · **[third-party]** a secondary source · **[inferred]** my conclusion from verified facts · **[unverified]** could not confirm.

## Short answer

- Of the providers with a 1,000,000-token window, **only Google Gemini and Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash take audio at all**. Anthropic's Claude (text and image only) and OpenAI's 1M-window models (text and image only) do not; OpenAI's audio models stop at 128,000 tokens. **[verified]**
- **Gemini mixes every input to mono and downsamples to 16 kbps**, so a Gemini model can never hear stereo width, and a 16 kbps codec removes most of what a mix engineer listens for above the midrange. **[verified]** for the first, **[inferred]** for the second. Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash is the one 1M model that documents keeping two channels (`use_multichannel`), at 7 tokens per second of audio. **[verified]**
- So the mentor cannot rely on the model's ears. **Measurements are the primary channel; audio is an optional attachment** that a provider may or may not accept. This matches the map's standing preference and turns it from a hedge into a requirement. **[inferred]**
- The numbers to compute first are the ones with a standard behind them and a known meaning to engineers: integrated, short-term and momentary loudness and loudness range (ITU-R BS.1770 / EBU R 128), true peak, crest factor, per-band level over time, stereo correlation and mid/side balance per band, and a spectral centroid. No paper shows a language model reasoning well over these features as text; the evidence that exists is for models fine-tuned on paired audio and text. That is why a numbers-versus-audio prototype is needed, and it is the recommendation at the end. See [Part 2](#part-2--numbers-that-can-stand-in-for-hearing).
- Compute them with **ffmpeg** (`ebur128`, `astats`, `aspectralstats`) when it is present, and plan a small pure-TypeScript fallback for BS.1770 loudness and band levels; the libraries in between either lack BS.1770 or carry a licence (AGPL) that does not fit an MIT repo. See [Part 3](#part-3--what-computes-the-numbers).

Reading the table:

- **Two providers qualify for audio, one of them keeps stereo.** Gemini is mono and 16 kbps by its own documentation. 16 kbps is the bitrate of narrowband voice codecs; it keeps speech intelligible and discards the top octaves and fine spectral detail, so judgements about air, sibilance, transient sharpness and stereo image are not available to any Gemini model, whatever its size. **[inferred]** Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash keeps two channels on request and its documentation says the point is to retain spatial information, which is exactly the stereo-field question. What it does to sample rate or bitrate is not documented. **[verified]** for the quotes, **[unverified]** for the quality.
- **Cost of a listen is small either way.** A 30-second window costs 960 tokens on Gemini (≈ $0.0003 on `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`) and 210 tokens on Qwen (≈ $0.00003). Over a session with a listen before and after every change, audio is cheaper than the measurement text that goes with it. **[inferred]** from the verified rates.
- **No provider documents music or mix understanding.** Gemini's doc names "emotion detection in speech and music" as a use case; everything else on every page is about speech. Whether any of these models can tell a muddy low end from a clean one from audio alone is not a documented capability and has to be tested. **[verified]** for the docs, **[inferred]** for the conclusion.
- **Claude cannot be on the audio path at all**, and OpenAI's audio-capable models fail the window rule. If the mentor required the model to hear, it would be a Gemini-or-Qwen product. The map's standing preference ("audio goes to the provider by default; measurements are always produced") keeps the model interchangeable: the harness attaches audio when the provider accepts it and drops it otherwise. **[inferred]**
- Gemini's **Live API** takes 16-bit PCM at 16 kHz, mono, which confirms the input resolution the audio understanding doc describes. **[verified]** The Vertex AI page for the same models says "Maximum audio length per prompt: Approximately 8.4 hours, or up to 1 million tokens; Maximum number of audio files per prompt: 1". **[verified]** One file per prompt matters for a tool result that wants to attach the Main and one track: on Vertex they would be two prompts. **[inferred]**

## Part 2 — numbers that can stand in for hearing

### What the evidence says about models reasoning over audio numbers

The honest finding: **no published work was found that tests a language model giving mix feedback from numeric audio descriptors written as text.** What exists is adjacent:

- **MixAssist** (COLM 2025) is the closest dataset: 431 audio-grounded turns from 7 mixing sessions between expert and amateur producers. Its models are audio-language models (a fine-tuned Qwen-Audio did best); the audio reaches the model as audio, not as numbers. **[paper]** [arXiv 2507.06329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06329)
- **LLM2Fx** (2025) shows a text-only LLM predicting EQ and reverb parameters zero-shot from a text description, and that giving it "audio Digital Signal Processing (DSP) features" as in-context examples improves the result. That is the one piece of evidence that DSP numbers in the prompt help a text model make a production decision. It is about choosing effect settings from a wish ("warmer"), not judging a mix from its measurements. **[paper]** [arXiv 2505.20770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20770)
- **Lexical acoustic coding** (2026) has an LLM analyse a waveform into "interpretable, non-learned acoustic descriptors", quantise them with a per-feature interval vocabulary and write them as English, which a second LLM turns back into sound. It reports that "plain text preserves measurable acoustic structure". That supports the idea that coarse, named intervals ("low-mid band: 6 dB above the mid band") are a form a model can use; it does not test mix judgement. **[paper]** [arXiv 2605.08750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08750)
- **MuChoMusic** (ISMIR 2024) found that audio-language models answering music questions show "an over-reliance on the language modality", that is, they answer from the text and not the audio. **[paper]** [arXiv 2408.01337](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01337). For this project that cuts both ways: it is a warning that a Gemini model given audio may still answer from the measurements and the prompt, and a hint that measurements are what the model actually uses. **[inferred]**
- **LLark** (ICML 2024) is a music-understanding model built on a pretrained music model plus an LLM; again audio in, not numbers. **[paper]** [LLark](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/gardner24a.html)

So the question "does a text model reason well over per-band loudness, crest factor and stereo correlation" is **open**, and the prototype on the map is the right way to settle it. **[inferred]**

### The numbers, and why an engineer trusts them

Ranked by how standard the number is and how directly it maps to a mix problem the mentor should find.

| Number | What it tells an engineer | Standard or source | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Integrated loudness** (LUFS) | Overall level of the window, K-weighted and gated so silence and quiet passages do not drag it down. The reference for "is it too quiet / too loud for the platform". | ITU-R BS.1770-5 (in force, 11/2023) defines the algorithm; EBU R 128 sets the −23 LUFS target with ±0.5 LU tolerance and the gating blocks of 400 ms with 75 % overlap and a relative gate of −10 LU **[verified]** | Compute first |
| **Short-term and momentary loudness over time**, with their maxima | How level moves through the window: a drop that drags on, a section that jumps. R 128 names "Maximum Momentary Loudness" and "Maximum Short-term Loudness" as measures to "further characterise an audio signal". **[verified]** | EBU Tech 3341 ("EBU Mode" meter) **[verified]**; the 3 s short-term window is in the same spec but was not re-read here **[unverified]** | Compute first, at 1 value per second |
| **Loudness range** (LRA, LU) | "The distribution of loudness within a programme": a squashed mix has a small LRA, a dynamic one a large one. **[verified]** | EBU Tech 3342 (named by libebur128 as what it implements) **[verified]** | Compute first |
| **True peak** (dBTP) | "The maximum value of the audio signal waveform of a programme in the continuous time domain": inter-sample clipping that a sample peak misses. **[verified]** | BS.1770 (over-sampled peak; ffmpeg's `ebur128` does "the peak lookup on an over-sampled version of the input stream") **[verified]** | Compute first |
| **Crest factor / peak-to-loudness ratio** (dB) | Peak over RMS; a low value means heavy limiting or compression, a high value means transients survived. | ffmpeg `astats` reports `Crest_factor` per channel **[verified]**; the engineering reading is standard practice **[inferred]** | Compute first (free from `astats`) |
| **Per-band level over time** (say 6 bands: sub < 60 Hz, low 60–250, low-mid 250–500, mid 500–2k, high-mid 2–6k, high > 6k) | Where the energy sits: a muddy low-mid, a missing sub, a harsh high-mid. The bands are the vocabulary engineers already use. | No standard for the bands; the per-band *level* is just RMS of a band-passed signal. **[inferred]** | Compute first; the most likely place a model finds a real problem **[inferred]** |
| **Stereo correlation and mid/side level, overall and per band** | Correlation near +1 is mono, near 0 is wide, negative means phase cancellation in mono playback; side level in the low band means a sub that will not translate. | No single standard; correlation is a textbook measurement (Pearson between L and R over a window). **[inferred]** | Compute first for the Main. This is the one thing *no Gemini model can hear at all* (mono mixdown) **[verified]** |
| **Spectral centroid, rolloff, flatness** | Brightness and tilt in one number each; `aspectralstats` gives centroid, spread, flatness, crest, flux, slope, rolloff per channel per frame. **[verified]** | ffmpeg `aspectralstats` **[verified]** | Compute second; cheap, but the per-band levels already say it in engineer's words **[inferred]** |
| **DC offset, clipping and flat-sample count** | `astats` gives `DC_offset`, `Flat_factor` (consecutive samples at the same level, a sign of clipping) and `Peak_count`. **[verified]** | ffmpeg `astats` **[verified]** | Compute first (free) |
| **Transient / onset list** | Where hits land and how hard; useful for "the kick is buried" questions. | No standard; an onset detector is more code and more choices than the rest. **[inferred]** | Later |
| **Coarse spectrogram as text** (bands × time, in dB) | The whole picture at once. The per-band-over-time rows above *are* a coarse spectrogram, so this is the same thing at a finer grid. | Lexical acoustic coding suggests interval words survive the trip into an LLM **[paper]** | Compute first as 6 bands × 1 s; go finer only if the prototype shows a model uses it **[inferred]** |
| **Comparison against a genre curve** | Whether the tonal balance is normal for the style. | Needs reference curves the project does not have; out of scope on the map (reference-track comparison is a later effort). | Later |

Size check against the 40,000-character result limit (ADR 0018): a 30 s window at 1 s resolution with momentary + short-term loudness (2 numbers), 6 band levels, correlation and side level in 2 bands (4 numbers) is 12 numbers × 30 rows ≈ 360 values; at ~6 characters each in a compact table that is ~2,500 characters, plus a dozen summary lines. Even a 2-minute window at 1 s stays under 10,000. **[inferred]**

## Recommendation for the numbers-versus-audio prototype

1. **Measurements are the channel; audio is an optional attachment.** Only Gemini and Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash can take audio among 1M-window models, Gemini hears mono at 16 kbps, and no provider documents music or mix understanding. A design that needs the model's ears would be a two-provider product; one that sends numbers works with every model on the list. **[verified]** for the facts, **[inferred]** for the design.
2. **The prototype compares three conditions on the planted-problem Set**: numbers only; numbers plus audio (on Gemini, and on Qwen with `use_multichannel: true` if a key is available); audio only. Score how often the mentor names each planted problem (a 6 dB low-mid boost on one track, a sub in the side channel, a limited drum bus with a crest factor under 6 dB, an inter-sample clip, a 4 dB level jump at a bar). Run each case several times, as AGENTS.md asks for evals. **[inferred]**
3. **First numbers to compute**, all from one ffmpeg call on the recorded WAV: integrated loudness, LRA, true peak, max momentary and short-term; momentary loudness per second; 6 band levels per second; crest factor, DC offset, flat factor and peak count per channel; stereo correlation and side level overall and for the sub/low band. Give each row a bar number from the `transport` position so the model can say *where*. Everything else in Part 2 waits for evidence that the model uses what it already has. **[inferred]**
4. **Format**: a short summary block in engineer's units, then one compact table row per second. Name the numbers as a mix engineer would ("low-mid 250–500 Hz: −18 dBFS") rather than as DSP variables; lexical acoustic coding and LLM2Fx both suggest words and intervals carry further than raw vectors. **[inferred]**
5. **What this changes on the map**: the requirement a model must meet to hear is not "accepts audio" but "is on the supported list"; audio becomes a provider capability flag in the adapter (Gemini: yes, mono; Qwen: yes, stereo on request; Anthropic and OpenAI 1M models: no). The README's plain statement that audio goes to the provider by default holds, with the honest footnote that today's provider hears it in mono. **[inferred]**
